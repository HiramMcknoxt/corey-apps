THE COMMITTEE ROOM IS EMPTY. GOOD.

Doing politics is the problem. Political parties are not identities. They are infrastructure: ballot lines, bylaws, bank accounts, endorsements — and rooms where a handful of people make binding decisions about all of it. In a lot of counties those rooms are understaffed. Some are effectively vacant. Nobody is guarding the door, because nobody thinks the room matters.

The room is the whole game. Find yours. Take a seat. Change the rule. Show the receipt.

The horizon: both candidates unbought. Imagine a general election where neither candidate took corporate PAC or lobbyist money. THE PLEBEIAN RECLAMATION

A dual-track, hostile-but-lawful takeover of local party committees from within — by the people the parties were built to represent.

The philosophy: Dynamic Synthesism

Efficacy over aesthetic. A policy is judged by whether it works, not by which tribe it flatters. Ideas from anywhere are tested, combined, and either kept, improved, or retired — with the verdict recorded in the Living Ledger. Leaders are graded on one axis: Sophistry vs. Stewardship — performing, or passing clean-government rules and doing the work? Parties are infrastructure, never identity. You do not identify with the water department. You show up to the meeting where its rates are set.

Phase 1 — Geographic Audits

Map the terrain county by county: committee rosters, seat counts, vacancy rates, meeting schedules, and the bylaws that govern each room. The audit turns a vague complaint (“politics is broken”) into a number (“this committee has 40 seats, 17 filled, and a quorum of 9”).

Phase 2 — Quorum & Seat Acquisition

Small cadres — three to five serious people — claim vacant precinct and committee seats by filing the form and attending the meetings. In a half-empty committee, a handful of reliable attendees is not a faction. It is a quorum problem for the existing machine, and a governing majority in waiting.

Phase 3 — The Clean-Money Floor Vote

Once seated, move the mechanism: a binding bylaw that cuts the committee, its auxiliaries, and its candidates off from money the committee itself designates as misaligned with its platform. Not a resolution. Not a pledge. A rule, adopted on the floor, with a public list, refunds, and an investigation process. Lonoke County did this on January 23, 2026.

An honest note on tracks: the mechanics work inside either party, in any county, because they operate on procedure rather than ideology. Corey Spangler's current operational home is the Democratic Party in Lonoke County, Arkansas, where he serves as county chair. That is disclosed, not hidden.

STEAL THIS RULE — THE MECHANISM

LABELED SUMMARY

What follows is a faithful summary of the adopted §601.4 designation mechanism — not the adopted text. This app does not reproduce the bylaw verbatim; the label is the product.

Designation, not categories. The County Committee designates specific entities — any organization, corporation, or private individual — as “grossly misaligned in their mission, message, or operations” with the party platform.

The ban binds everyone the committee controls. The committee itself, its auxiliaries, and its own candidates and nominees may not accept funds from a designated entity.

The list is public and maintained. The Secretary keeps the proscribed list, publicly available and distributed to candidates. Additions and removals are proposed from the floor and voted at the following meeting by simple majority.

Mistakes get unwound. A contribution from a designated entity is refunded within 30 days. The Rules Committee investigates suspected violations and recommends remediation.

Why this design? A fixed list of banned categories goes stale or gets lawyered around. A designation model lets the membership itself decide, in public, whose money is incompatible with its stated platform — and makes that decision revisable by the same body that made it.

RUN YOUR OWN COUNTY AUDIT

1. Find your committee. Every county has (or is supposed to have) a county party committee for each party. Find when and where yours meets, and who the chair and secretary are. If nobody can tell you, that is your first finding.

2. Get the roster and the bylaws. The roster tells you how many seats exist and how many are filled. The bylaws tell you how seats are claimed, what quorum is, and how rules get changed. Ask politely. Ask in writing if you have to.

3. Count the chairs. Total seats, filled seats, vacant seats. Then do the quorum math in Take a Seat.

4. Attend once as an observer. Show up, sit down, take notes. Learn the procedure before you try to use it.

5. Claim a seat — and bring a quorum. File whatever the bylaws and state law require to fill a vacant seat for your precinct. Recruit to the number the math gave you, and put the clean-money rule on the agenda under New Business.

PREMISE CHECK — THE RHETORICAL LOGIC AUDIT

We autopsy the argument, not the person. One political line at a time, slowed down and rebuilt where everyone can see the moving parts.

The method

Reconstruct the argument as premises and a conclusion, in the strongest form the speaker's words support. Expose the hidden premise. Name the fallacy — if there is one; if the honest reconstruction is not fallacious, we pick a different clip. Steelman the other side before grading anything. Verdict on Sophistry vs. Stewardship. The same test applies to everyone. Naming a fallacy does not prove a conclusion false — it proves that argument did not prove it.

House rules

Reconstruct with the speaker's evident meaning — never literalize a figurative line. Steelman first, at full strength; then the verdict doesn't pull punches: name absurdity and internal contradiction plainly, and hunt position bundles that undercut their own stated goal. Quotes are verified against a reliable source before anything airs; unverifiable quotes are marked paraphrase, never put in quotation marks. AI narration is disclosed on every episode.

Three formats — don't bolt them together

1 · Logic Audit — the full autopsy: premises, validity, hidden premise, fallacy, steelman, verdict.
2 · Lie Exposure — no logic breakdown: the claim, the verified facts that refute it with sources, and what the lie was doing.
3 · Fallacy Spotlight — one named fallacy, taught plainly: definition, skeleton, and a verified modern example.
